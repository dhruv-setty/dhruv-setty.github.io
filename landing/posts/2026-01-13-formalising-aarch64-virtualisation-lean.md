## Context

Invariant: a guest shall reach only the memory assigned to it, and after the hypervisor reclaims a page, no core shall reach that page. On AArch64 the invariant is held jointly by the Stage 2 translation tables written at EL2 and by the TLB entries that cache them.

IATO v7 defines, verifies and implements Stage 2 translation and TLB maintenance for a hypervisor at EL2. v0.1.0 was a Lean 4 review of the specification against DDI 0487K, DDI 0608A, KVM and QEMU, preserved at the tag `archive/lean-v0.1.0`. v0.2.0 replaces review with executable artefacts:

| Part | Artefact | Checked by |
|---|---|---|
| Normative reference | `spec/sail/stage2.sail`, `spec/sail/tlbi_scope.sail`, compiled to C | 8 Sail assertions, an independent Python walker |
| Stage 2 walker | `rtl/s2_walker.sv` | Sail reference and Python walker, Verilator cross-check, Yosys |
| Stage 2 TLB | `rtl/s2_tlb.sv` | Icarus testbench with 13 checks, Sail TLBI scope assertions, Yosys |
| Unmap ordering | `spec/tla/S2Unmap.tla`, three configurations | TLC |

Scope: 4 KiB granule, 48 bit IPA, start level 0; Stage 2 only and combined TLB entries tagged by VMID and ASID; TLBI IPAS2E1IS, VMALLE1IS, VMALLS12E1IS and VAE1IS; unmap of one IPA page across two cores. The artefacts do not constitute a complete hypervisor or MMU. Every figure was generated by the captures workflow on an `ubuntu-24.04` runner; schematics are drawn by Yosys and netlistsvg, and all other figures are screenshots of the named tool.

## Design rationale

### Normative reference

The Sail model is normative: every walker check is a comparison against it. The Sail model shall itself be checked against an independent Python walker, since a reference that agrees only with itself establishes nothing. The probe generator constructs random tables containing pages, 2 MiB and 1 GiB blocks, invalid and reserved descriptors, clear access flags and every S2AP value, and probes inside and outside each mapping. The probe mix shall be fault dominated.

### Walker requirements

- **S2-W-01.** Resolve a 48 bit IPA in at most four levels, nine IPA bits per level.
- **S2-W-02 to S2-W-05.** Translation, reserved descriptor, access flag and permission faults.
- **S2-W-06.** Result word: bit 63 fault, bits 62 to 60 kind, bits 59 to 58 level, bits 47 to 0 output address; identical to the Sail encoding.

The walker shall not update the access flag. It holds one table read in flight; a full walk costs `1 + 4 × (latency + 1)` cycles.

### TLB lookup and scope requirements

Lookups match on VMID; combined lookups additionally match on ASID unless the entry is global.

| ID | Operation | The TLB removes |
|---|---|---|
| S2-T-01 | IPAS2E1IS | Stage 2 only entries of the VMID for the IPA page |
| S2-T-02 | VMALLE1IS | Combined entries of the VMID |
| S2-T-03 | VMALLS12E1IS | All entries of the VMID |
| S2-T-04 | VAE1IS | Combined entries of the VMID for the VA page, with a matching ASID or the global attribute |
| S2-T-05 | Any operation | No entry of any other VMID |

### Unmap ordering

S2-U-01: clear the descriptor, DSB, TLBI IPAS2E1IS, DSB, TLBI VMALLE1IS, DSB; the frame shall be freed only after the final DSB.

The TLA+ model has two cores. Each may cache a Stage 2 entry (`S2`) and a combined entry (`CB`), and may refill a combined entry from a cached Stage 2 entry without a table read. Broadcast TLBIs complete at the next DSB. Safety property `NoUseAfterFree`: no guest access reaches the frame after it is freed.

## Observations

- The probe mix is fault dominated, as a conformance suite requires; mean cycles per walk fall below the four level figure because most faulting probes terminate at an early level.
- Each walk level is one serialised memory read (Figure 5); walk latency scales linearly with memory latency.
- The four TLB fills give each scope an entry to remove and an entry to retain: a different VMID, a different entry type and a global entry. The `kill` pattern per operation matches the scope table in hardware.
- IPAS2E1IS removes Stage 2 only entries and retains combined entries (S2-T-01). Both TLC counterexamples depend on this: both broken sequences issue the correct instructions, one omitting a step and the other swapping two. Instruction order in the unmap is a correctness requirement, not an optimisation.
- The Icarus warning `sorry: Case unique/unique0 qualities are ignored` applies to `rtl/s2_tlb.sv`; Verilator lints the same file clean (Figure 12).
- An open tool synthesis result is meaningful only with its tool versions attached.

## Limitations

- Simulation and open tool synthesis only; no physical FPGA or silicon measurement.
- The TLA+ result holds for one IPA page and two cores. The model does not cover memory ordering beyond DSB completion.
- Both synthesis results are out of context estimates on a Lattice LFE5U-25F, not vendor tool or ASIC figures. The walker and TLB are synthesised as separate blocks; integrated timing is not measured.
- FEAT_HAFDBS (hardware access flag and dirty state update) is out of scope.
- Not implemented: a cross-check of the walker and TLBI scopes against a QEMU TCG guest at EL2; overlapped walks and cached level 0 to 2 descriptors, measured again at latency 32; concatenated start tables with a 40 bit IPA and start level 1, and FEAT_HAFDBS; the walker, TLB and TLBI unit integrated and synthesised as one block.
